How We Started

A Steakhouse Built Around One Fire

Ember & Oak opened in 2016 with a simple idea: the best steakhouse in Seattle shouldn't feel like every other steakhouse. Founder Daniel Okafor spent a decade cooking over open flame across the Pacific Northwest before deciding that a single live-fire hearth — no gas, no shortcuts — would be the heart of his own restaurant.

What started as a 40-seat dining room on Western Avenue has grown into one of downtown Seattle's most requested tables, without ever installing a second fire source.

Milestones

Eight Years by the Fire

2016

Ember & Oak Opens

A 40-seat dining room opens on Western Avenue with a single live-fire hearth and a 12-bottle wine list.

2018

Private Dining Debuts

The Hearth Room opens next door, adding two private spaces for groups of 10 to 40.

2021

In-House Dry-Age Cellar

A climate-controlled aging cellar is built on site, allowing cuts to dry-age up to 45 days.

2024

Regional Recognition

Named among Seattle Met's "Essential Steakhouses" and expands the wine program past 120 labels.

Today

Still One Fire

Every steak that leaves the kitchen still touches the same live-fire hearth as it did in 2016.

Sourcing

Rooted in the Pacific Northwest

Our produce comes from small farms across Washington and Oregon, our seafood from day-boat fishers on the Sound, and our beef from ranch partners we visit in person every season.

  • Direct relationships with 6 regional farms
  • Sustainable, day-boat Pacific seafood
  • Beef from Pacific Northwest cattle ranches
